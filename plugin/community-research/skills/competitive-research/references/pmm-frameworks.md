# Product Marketing Frameworks for Reddit Evidence

## Attribute set

Code each product mention against one of these attributes, and add attributes specific to the category where needed:

`price/value` · `ease of use` · `onboarding` · `core features` · `advanced features` · `integrations` · `reliability` · `performance` · `support` · `security/compliance` · `scalability` · `ecosystem/community` · `company trust`

## Strengths and weaknesses grid

Rows are attributes and columns are products. In each cell, put `+`, `−`, `±` (mixed) or blank (not discussed), with the number of mentions. Only mark a cell when there are at least 3 independent mentions. Otherwise write "thin".

## Perception map

Choose the two attributes that best separate the products in the evidence, often price/value against power or ease. Place each product in one quadrant, and justify each placement with one quote. Describe the map in text or as a small table. Don't invent coordinates.

## Switching drivers

For each direction (A → B), record the trigger event (price change, outage, missing feature, acquisition), the pull factor (what B offered), and the count. Triggers show what the competitor is weak on. Pull factors show what the market values.

## Customer language

- Collect the exact nouns and verbs users use for the problem ("keeping docs in sync", not "knowledge management").
- Note which words carry emotion (fear, relief, frustration) and which describe outcomes.
- Flag gaps between vendor jargon and user vocabulary. These are chances to rewrite messaging in the customer's words.

## Positioning gap test

An opportunity counts only when all three are true:
1. **Need:** the need appears in several threads, from different users.
2. **Gap:** no product in the set is consistently praised for meeting it.
3. **Credibility:** the user's product could credibly own it. If you can't judge this, flag it as a question for the user.

## Battlecard template (one per competitor)

```markdown
## Battlecard: <Competitor>
**What users say they're good at:** <2–3 points, with quotes>
**Where users say they fall short:** <2–3 points, with quotes and links>
**Why people leave them:** <top switching triggers>
**Why people choose them:** <top pull factors>
**Landmines to set:** <questions a prospect can ask that bring out the weaknesses, grounded in the evidence>
**Objections to expect:** <what their fans say against alternatives>
**Evidence base:** <n threads, n comments, date range>
```

Every battlecard claim must link to a source. Leave a claim out rather than state it without evidence.
